Which CBT apps still exist?

Several “best CBT apps of 2026” lists recommend apps that closed in 2024 and 2025. This is what the App Store returned when somebody actually checked.

Why this page exists

Two apps that appear on current roundups, and on university and charity resource pages, cannot be used by a member of the public who follows the link:

  • Woebot — Woebot Health retired the consumer app on 30 June 2025 and moved to an enterprise model; access now needs a code from a partner organisation. Reported at the time by MobiHealthNews and STAT, and described in Woebot Health’s own FAQ.
    The two platforms have since diverged, which is worth knowing if you maintain a page that links both. On Android it is simply gone: on 25 August 2026 both play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.woebot and the Woebot Health Inc developer page returned 404, checked three times, while a control app returned 200 on the same request. On iOS the listing still exists, which is exactly what lets a broken recommendation survive a link check.
  • Sanvello, formerly Pacifica — removed from the App Store and Google Play in June 2024, and now AbleTo SelfCare+, open to members only.
  • Youper — not gone yet, but going. Its own homepage says “Youper is shutting down September 30, 2026 — export your data”, and the company states that accounts, chat histories and personal data are permanently deleted from its servers and backups from 1 October. The export route is start.youper.ai/export-data, which loads as of 25 August 2026. Full detail, including what happens on 1 October and what to use instead, is on the Youper page.
    If you recommend it anywhere, the useful response is probably not to delete the entry but to leave it up briefly with a line about the export. Someone who never sees the notice loses their writing. It is also already out of the App Store, so it does not turn up in a search — which means people who still have it installed may not know any of this is happening.

Nobody maintains a list of which of these tools are still alive, so stale recommendations sit on trusted pages for years. This is an attempt at one.

A recent date means somebody shipped a build. It does not mean the app is good, and an old date does not mean it is broken — a finished tool can sit still. What it tells you is whether anyone is still there.

What could not be confirmed

Sintelly, Bloom, Thought Challenger, Togetherall, and the original Thriveport MoodKit (a US listing exists under that name but with a different publisher), did not come back on a US store search under those names. That is not evidence that they are gone. A search misses apps for renaming, regional availability, or a store title that differs from what people call it.

They are listed as unchecked rather than quietly dropped. A list that hides its gaps is worse than no list. Happify used to sit here and no longer does: it is in the table above as Dario Mind (Twill), which is published by Happify, Inc. under the bundle com.happify.kabinet and describes itself as formerly Twill. Renamed, not gone — which is why a name search alone was never enough to say.

Method, and its limits

  • Apple’s public iTunes Search API, US storefront, 25 August 2026. Every name, publisher and date above comes straight from that response.
  • Rows were matched by hand. An automated match put “Pacifica” against a high school’s app on the first pass — which is precisely the error this page would otherwise have shipped.
  • The two withdrawals are the only claims resting on anything other than the store, and both are supported by contemporaneous reporting and by the companies’ own pages.
  • One storefront. Availability differs by country.
  • This is a snapshot, and it will go stale — which is the problem it describes. The check date sits above the table for that reason.

Relief is in the table because leaving it out would have been the tell. If something here is wrong, it is worth more to me corrected than flattering — tell me and I will change it and say the date it changed.
