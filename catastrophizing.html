Catastrophizing

Believing that what has happened or will happen will be so awful and unbearable that you won't be able to stand it.

Why it happens

Catastrophizing involves anticipating the worst possible outcome, even when it is highly unlikely. It is an evolutionary mechanism gone awry: our brains attempt to protect us by preparing for the absolute worst, but in modern contexts this often leads to paralyzing anxiety over everyday events.

It typically manifests as an escalating chain of “what if” thoughts, rapidly progressing from a minor issue to a life-altering disaster.

In real situations

Professional

“My boss didn't say hi to me this morning. He must be angry about my report. I'm going to get fired and lose my apartment.”

The boss might be distracted, stressed about their own work, or simply didn't see you. One missed greeting rarely correlates with imminent termination.

Personal

“My partner is 30 minutes late and hasn't texted. They must have been in a terrible car crash. I need to call the hospitals.”

Traffic, a dead phone battery, or losing track of time are statistically far more common explanations than a catastrophic accident.

What it sounds like

  • If I make one mistake, everyone will lose trust in me.
  • This symptom means something is seriously wrong.
  • If I say no, this opportunity is gone forever.

Challenge Protocol

Use this sequence to test the worst-case story before it becomes your reality.

  • Evaluate Probability — Ask yourself: “What are the actual odds of this worst-case scenario happening?” Contrast this with the most likely outcome based on evidence.
  • Assess Coping Capacity — Even if the difficult thing happens, remind yourself of past resilience. Ask what actionable steps you would take next.
  • Halt the Chain — Notice the “what if” spiral and intentionally stop it. Bring attention back to the immediate facts you actually know right now.

Quick checks

  • Ask for the most likely outcome.
  • Name one manageable next step.
  • Estimate probability, not fear intensity.

Ask yourself: What is the most likely outcome if I zoom out?

A kinder version: This is uncomfortable, not catastrophic. I can handle the next step.
