Discounting the Positive

Dismissing good things as flukes or irrelevant.

Why it happens

Discounting the positive filters out evidence that would challenge a negative self-story. Wins are minimized, explained away, or dismissed before they can update your beliefs.

The distortion keeps the old narrative intact by treating confirming evidence as meaningful and disconfirming evidence as irrelevant or suspicious.

In real situations

Professional

“They only complimented my work to be polite. It does not actually mean I did anything well.”

Positive feedback is still data. Even if kindness is present, it does not cancel the possibility that your work genuinely landed well.

Personal

“I handled that conversation calmly, but it doesn't count because anyone should be able to do that.”

A strength does not become meaningless just because it looks ordinary from the inside. Calm responses often reflect real growth and effort.

What it sounds like

  • They only praised me to be nice.
  • That success does not count; it was luck.
  • I did well, but anyone could have done it.

Let the Evidence Count

Practice taking positive data seriously enough that it can update the story you tell yourself.

  • Capture the Specific Positive — Name exactly what went well instead of speaking about it vaguely or dismissing it immediately.
  • Identify the Skill or Effort — Ask what ability, decision, or persistence helped create the outcome rather than defaulting to luck alone.
  • Integrate the Evidence — Write one balanced sentence that allows the positive fact to coexist with your imperfections.

Quick checks

  • Treat positive data as real evidence.
  • Track wins and effort, not only outcomes.
  • Ask what this says about your actual capabilities.

Ask yourself: What positive data am I deleting right now?

A kinder version: This success counts, even if it was not perfect.
