The thought record

The core worksheet of cognitive behavioural therapy. Seven columns, one thought at a time. Here is what each column is for, a filled-in example, and a blank you can copy.

What it is

A thought record is a structured way of writing down one distressing thought and testing whether it holds up. It has been the backbone of CBT homework for about fifty years, and it is deliberately boring: the same columns, every time, so the structure does the remembering and you only have to do the thinking.

Therapists set them between sessions. You do not need a therapist to use one.

The columns, and what each is actually for

SituationWhere you were and what happened. Facts only — a camera would agree with this row.
Automatic thoughtThe thought, in the words it arrived in. Not tidied up.
Distress (0–10)How bad it feels right now. You will compare against this later.
DistortionWhich thinking trap the thought is using. Naming it turns a fact about the world into a habit of thought.
Evidence forWhat genuinely supports the thought. Do this column first and honestly, or the exercise becomes an argument you have rigged.
Evidence againstWhat genuinely argues the other way. This is the column that does the work.
Balanced thoughtA version that accounts for both columns. Fairer, not more positive.
Distress again (0–10)Re-rate. It may not have moved — that is information, not failure.

A filled-in example

SituationSent a message to a friend on Tuesday morning. No reply by Wednesday night.
Automatic thought“She's had enough of me. I always do this to people.”
Distress8
DistortionMind reading, and all-or-nothing thinking in “always”.
Evidence forShe usually replies within a day. The last thing I sent was a bit long.
Evidence againstShe had a deadline this week and said so. She replied warmly last time. “Always” is not true — I can think of three friendships that have lasted years.
Balanced thought“She's probably busy. If something is wrong I'd rather ask than decide it on her behalf.”
Distress again4

Note the balanced thought is not “everything is fine”. It leaves the possibility open and names an action. That is what makes it hold up tomorrow.
