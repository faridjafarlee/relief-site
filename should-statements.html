Should Statements

Rigid rules about how things must be.

Why it happens

Should statements create rigid rules for yourself, for other people, and for reality. When life inevitably fails to obey those rules, the result is often shame, resentment, or chronic pressure.

Because these rules can sound responsible or moral, the distortion often goes unnoticed even while it increases emotional suffering.

In real situations

Professional

“I should always be productive. If I need a slow afternoon, I am falling behind everyone else.”

Human energy naturally fluctuates. Sustainable performance includes recovery, recalibration, and variable pace.

Personal

“My partner should know what I need without me having to say it. If I have to ask, something is wrong with the relationship.”

Healthy relationships still rely on communication. Unspoken expectations are not a fair measure of care.

What it sounds like

  • I should always be productive.
  • I should never need help.
  • They should know what I need without me saying it.

Loosen the Rule

Translate rigid demands into preferences and realistic standards that a human being can actually live by.

  • Identify the Hidden Rule — Write the sentence out fully. Notice whether it sounds like a demand, a law, or a threat.
  • Replace “Should” with “Prefer” — Shift the language from rigid obligation to flexible preference without abandoning what matters to you.
  • Choose a Humane Standard — Ask whether the rule is realistic, kind, and sustainable for a real person in real circumstances.

Quick checks

  • Swap should with prefer.
  • Check whether the rule is realistic.
  • Choose compassionate standards.

Ask yourself: Is this rule humane and sustainable?

A kinder version: I prefer this outcome, but I can be flexible and still effective.
